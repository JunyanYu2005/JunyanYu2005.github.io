I am Junyan Yu, a final-year undergraduate at The Chinese University of Hong Kong ([CUHK](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/english/index.html)), majoring in [Chinese Language and Literature](https://www.chi.cuhk.edu.hk/en/) with a minor in [Linguistics](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/).

My research interests lie in Chinese dialect grammar, particularly the syntax, semantics, and pragmatics of aspect. My current work investigates progressive and durative expressions in Shaoxing Wu, examining how grammatical realization interacts with lexical meaning, discourse context, and sentence type. More broadly, I am interested in how empirical evidence from Sinitic varieties informs linguistic theory and our understanding of grammatical structure.

Alongside formal and dialectal linguistics, I have a strong foundation in Chinese literature and traditional philology. Training in these fields has honed my sensitivity to textual evidence, historical context, and close reading—a methodological rigor I aim to integrate into my future research in Chinese linguistics.

Complementing my research, I am keenly interested in Chinese language education and writing pedagogy. Informed by my background in linguistics and literary studies, I focus on helping learners cultivate analytical reading, clear academic and creative writing, and metalinguistic awareness across diverse Chinese-speaking contexts.

This website provides an overview of my academic background, research projects, conference presentations, and teaching experience. I warmly welcome correspondence and scholarly exchange with anyone working on Chinese linguistics, literature, or language education.
